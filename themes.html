Research themes

Four connected research directions from the accepted proposal. Across them, interactional fidelity and user heterogeneity are central to understanding when simulated users are valid proxies for real users.

1

Capabilities and Uses

What can simulated users reliably do?

Relevant uses include data generation, system training, failure discovery, benchmarking, personalization, and scientific inquiry.

  • Controllability and generalization across systems and domains.
  • Offline uses and interactive settings in which user and system behaviors evolve together.
  • Simulation through text, speech, multimodal interfaces, and tools.
2

Human–Simulator Gaps

Which differences matter?

Which differences between people and simulators matter for a given use? We focus on behavior over time and on variation beyond an “average” user.

  • Interactional fidelity: whether simulators preserve clarification, selective disclosure, state changes, goal revision, and abandonment over time. Fluent responses are insufficient when roles or goals drift.
  • User heterogeneity: variation across language, culture, accessibility, expertise, preferences, and user state.
  • Within-group and intersectional variation, rare goals and behaviors, accessibility needs, and changing user states that average-user simulators often erase.
3

Evaluation and Validation

What evidence makes a simulator trustworthy?

Because simulator validity is use-dependent, evaluation must match the claim a simulator is meant to support, whether about interaction trajectories, population distributions, rare events, or downstream system rankings.

  • Human evidence appropriate to the intended use and target users.
  • Validation units and uncertainty.
  • Transfer assumptions and sim-to-real gaps.
4

Responsible and Real-World Use

When should simulation inform decisions?

This direction addresses privacy, consent, stereotyping, transparency, misuse, and the limits of replacing human participation.

  • Simulated users that complement, rather than silently substitute for, affected communities.
  • Fit-for-purpose human validation, especially for underrepresented populations and consequential decisions.

Scope

The workshop includes offline and interactive uses of user simulation. Multi-agent work is in scope when its primary object is user behavior or user-facing AI. Macro-scale social simulation is outside the workshop’s scope.
