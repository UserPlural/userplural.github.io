Research themes

The workshop is organized around four connected research directions from the accepted proposal. Across these directions, interactional fidelity and user heterogeneity are central to understanding when simulated users are valid proxies for real users.

1. Capabilities and Uses

What can simulated users reliably do? Relevant uses include data generation, system training, failure discovery, benchmarking, personalization, and scientific inquiry.

  • Controllability and generalization across systems and domains.
  • Offline uses and interactive settings in which user and system behaviors evolve together.
  • Simulation through text, speech, multimodal interfaces, and tools.

2. Human–Simulator Gaps

Which differences between people and simulators matter for a given use? We focus on behavior over time and variation beyond an “average” user.

  • Interactional fidelity: clarification, selective disclosure, state changes, goal revision, and abandonment.
  • User heterogeneity: variation across language, culture, accessibility, expertise, preferences, and user state.
  • Within-group and intersectional variation, rare goals, and long-tail behaviors.

3. Evaluation and Validation

What evidence supports a simulator’s intended use? Evaluation should match the claim being made about interaction trajectories, population distributions, rare events, or downstream system rankings.

  • Human evidence appropriate to the intended use and target users.
  • Validation units and uncertainty.
  • Transfer assumptions, sim-to-real gaps, and mismatches that could change a conclusion.

4. Responsible and Real-World Use

When should simulation inform decisions? This direction addresses privacy, consent, stereotyping, transparency, misuse, and the limits of replacing human participation.

  • Human validation involving affected communities.
  • Underrepresented users and consequential decisions.
  • Simulation that complements human participation, with its limitations made explicit.

Scope

The workshop includes offline and interactive uses of user simulation. Multi-agent work is in scope when its primary object is user behavior or user-facing AI. Macro-scale social simulation is outside the workshop’s scope.
