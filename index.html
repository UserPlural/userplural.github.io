01 / Overview

There is more than
one kind of user.

Real users clarify, withhold information, correct misunderstandings, change goals, and abandon tasks. User simulation offers a scalable way to generate training data, probe system behavior, discover failures, and support repeatable evaluation—but fluency is not validity.

The User, Plural brings two questions into focus: interactional fidelity—whether simulators preserve clarification, selective disclosure, state changes, goal revision, and abandonment over time—and user heterogeneity—whether they capture meaningful variation and long-tail behavior across language, culture, accessibility, expertise, preferences, and user state.

Validity is necessarily fit-for-purpose: evidence sufficient for data generation may not support system ranking, population inference, or consequential decisions. We ask how intended use should determine the human evidence, validation unit, uncertainty, and transfer assumptions needed to support a claim.

This first workshop edition builds on the ACL 2026 User Simulation Birds-of-a-Feather session and its continuing working group. We bring together NLP, HCI, behavioral science, psychology, and responsible-AI perspectives to develop a shared research agenda and a practical reporting checklist covering simulator purpose, target users, validation, and limitations.

02 / Research themes

Interaction. Heterogeneity.
Evidence.

Four connected research directions,
grounded in the diversity of real users.

01

Capabilities and Uses

What can simulated users reliably do? We welcome work on data generation, system training, failure discovery, benchmarking, personalization, and scientific inquiry.

  • Controllability and generalization across systems and domains
  • Offline uses and evolving user–system interactions
  • Text, speech, multimodal interfaces, and tools
02

Human–Simulator Gaps

Which differences matter? Interactional fidelity and user heterogeneity are central: simulators must preserve meaningful behavior over time and represent variation beyond an “average” user.

  • Clarification, selective disclosure, goal revision, and abandonment
  • Within-group variation, intersections, and rare behaviors
  • Accessibility needs and changing user states
03

Evaluation and Validation

What evidence makes a simulator trustworthy? Evaluation should match the claim being made about interaction trajectories, population distributions, rare events, or downstream system rankings.

  • Human evidence appropriate to the intended use
  • Validation units and uncertainty
  • Transfer assumptions and sim-to-real gaps
04

Responsible and Real-World Use

When should simulation inform decisions? We invite work on privacy, consent, stereotyping, transparency, misuse, and the limits of replacing human participation.

  • Human validation involving affected communities
  • Underrepresented users and consequential decisions
  • Simulation that complements human participation
A shared question

Which mismatch between simulated and real users could change the conclusion?

Research scope

We welcome work on user simulation for developing, evaluating, and studying AI systems, including offline uses and interactive settings where user and system behaviors evolve together through text, speech, multimodal interfaces, or tools.

  • State and strategy modeling; multi-turn and multimodal simulation
  • Multilingual, multidialectal, culturally grounded, low-resource, accessibility-aware, and intersectional users
  • Rare-behavior stress testing and human–simulator gaps
  • Sim-to-real transfer and controllable representations
  • Datasets, metrics, and responsible deployment

Scope boundary: Multi-agent work is in scope when its primary object is user behavior or user-facing AI. Macro-scale social simulation is outside the workshop’s scope.

This scope follows the accepted proposal. Submission categories and the final call are still being prepared.
