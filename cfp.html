Workshop scope

The User, Plural: Heterogeneity and Interaction in User Simulation examines when, how, and for what purposes simulated users can serve as valid proxies for real users. Its central concerns are interactional fidelity, user heterogeneity, and evidence appropriate to a simulator’s intended use.

The research scope covers user simulation for developing, evaluating, and studying AI systems, including offline uses and interactive settings where user and system behaviors evolve together through text, speech, multimodal interfaces, or tools.

Topics of interest

  • State and strategy modeling; multi-turn and multimodal simulation.
  • Multilingual, multidialectal, culturally grounded, low-resource, accessibility-aware, and intersectional users.
  • Rare-behavior stress testing and human–simulator gaps.
  • Sim-to-real transfer and controllable representations.
  • Datasets, metrics, and responsible deployment.

These topics span the proposal’s four research directions: Capabilities and Uses; Human–Simulator Gaps; Evaluation and Validation; and Responsible and Real-World Use.

Scope boundary: Multi-agent work is in scope when its primary object is user behavior or user-facing AI. Macro-scale social simulation is outside the workshop’s scope.
